# Anti-bias Rules

## Confirmation bias

For every important assumption, actively seek evidence that would make it false.

## Founder-build bias

The ability to build quickly is not evidence that building is the right experiment.

## Solution anchoring

Research the underlying job and existing behavior before optimizing the proposed solution.

## Market-size substitution

A large parent market does not prove demand for the proposed wedge.

## Competitor paradox

Competition can indicate demand, but can also indicate the problem is already solved well enough. Investigate switching conditions.

## Interview politeness

Positive interview reactions are weak evidence unless tied to concrete past behavior, budget, or commitment.

## Survivorship bias

Search for abandoned products, failed approaches, discontinued features, complaints, and low-retention categories.

## Novelty bias

"Nobody does this" may indicate opportunity or lack of demand. Treat it as an unknown, not a moat.

## AI feature bias

AI capability is not customer value by itself. The venture must still solve a valuable job with acceptable reliability, cost, and workflow fit.
