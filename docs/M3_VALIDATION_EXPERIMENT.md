# M3 external validation experiment

Status: **pre-registered before external cohort results are interpreted**.

The purpose of M3 is not to prove that Venture OS is a good product. It is to learn whether external users can complete the evidence-first workflow without the author carrying them through it, and whether the workflow changes real decisions.

## Cohort

Recruit at least 10 people who did not build Venture OS and who have a real idea, side project, product decision, or venture to evaluate.

Suggested mix:

- founders / indie hackers;
- software engineers with side projects;
- product, growth, research, or consulting practitioners.

Do not count the author or anyone who already knows the internal workflow well.

## Protocol

Each participant receives the public repository and one instruction:

> Follow the Quickstart with a real idea without live guidance. Use the normal issue/feedback surfaces if you get blocked.

Do not rescue a participant during the attempt. Help after the attempt may be useful, but the unaided result is the onboarding observation.

## Observations to record per attempt

- Quickstart attempted;
- venture created;
- research reached;
- first decision reached;
- experiment designed;
- experiment executed, when practical during the observation window;
- time to first decision;
- blocking step, if any;
- whether the workflow changed, delayed, narrowed, or cancelled a real next action;
- whether the participant needed author intervention;
- explicit request for a hosted/persistent/collaborative experience, if volunteered.

Do not substitute stars, likes, impressions, compliments, or stated intent for workflow completion.

## Pre-registered interpretation rules

These thresholds are diagnostic gates for this 10-person cohort, not universal product metrics.

### Continue the current workflow direction

Treat onboarding + decision usefulness as sufficiently promising for the next milestone when all are true:

- at least 7/10 create a venture without live help;
- at least 5/10 reach a first decision;
- at least 3/10 reach an experiment design;
- at least 3/10 report a concrete change to a real next action that can be tied to the workflow.

This does **not** establish willingness to pay or justify a hosted product by itself.

### Simplify onboarding before expanding the method

Prioritize onboarding/UX fixes when either is true:

- more than 4/10 fail before reaching research; or
- more than 3/10 require author intervention to understand the canonical flow.

Do not respond by adding more methodology unless the observed blocker is methodological rather than operational.

### Investigate decision quality before adding product surface

If at least 5/10 reach a decision but fewer than 3/10 can identify a concrete changed next action, treat the main problem as decision usefulness rather than onboarding.

Review gate quality, evidence handling, and whether the output is materially different from generic LLM advice.

### Investigate the learning loop

If at least 5/10 reach a decision but fewer than 3/10 reach an experiment design, inspect the decision → experiment transition before building additional workflow stages.

### Hosted-product signal

Treat hosted demand only as an exploration signal when multiple external users independently ask for persistence, collaboration, easier setup, or a non-local interface after successfully using the workflow.

Do not infer willingness to pay from feature requests. Validate that separately.

## Integrity rule

Do not change these thresholds after seeing cohort results. If the protocol is materially flawed, record the flaw and create a new validation experiment rather than rewriting this one.

## M3 decision

After at least 10 unaided attempts, record one explicit decision:

- **PROCEED** — workflow adoption and decision usefulness meet the pre-registered gates;
- **TEST** — evidence is mixed or a specific onboarding/decision-loop uncertainty needs another bounded experiment;
- **PARK** — external behavior does not justify continued investment under the current thesis.

Preserve contradictory feedback and failed attempts in the retrospective.
